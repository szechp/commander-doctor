# Strategic acceptance examples

These are expected reasoning boundaries for the assistant, not engine-predicted outcomes. The automated fixtures exercise the evidence and rejection boundaries; human review still evaluates whether a recommendation is persuasive.

## City on Fire versus Collective Inferno

Local Oracle snapshot: City costs {5}{R}{R}{R}, has convoke, and triples damage from your sources. Collective costs {3}{R}{R}, has convoke, and doubles damage from sources you control of one chosen creature type.

For a creature-type-focused list with an expensive curve, Collective is a plausible better fit: less printed mana and one fewer required red symbol, but a smaller multiplier and narrower source scope. For a plan relying on damage from enchantments, spells and creatures of several types, City's broader scope can matter more. Count relevant actual sources, not just creatures sharing a type. Neither is a direct upgrade; no winner follows from the mana value alone.

Convoke requires available creatures and appropriate colours. Tapping attackers has an opportunity cost. Creature density does not prove an available board on turn N. The six-draw test cannot establish which enchantment is cast first or wins more often. Ask whether this is a finishing slot, whether damage from other source types is essential, and whether the user prioritizes earlier deployment or maximum payoff.

## Same effect, lower generic cost

Synthetic fixture: two normal instants with identical full rules text and identical coloured demand; candidate costs one less generic mana. The helper may label a scoped direct upgrade. The assistant checks mana-value-specific tutors, recursion, payoffs and budget before recommending the replacement. Different target restrictions, speed, types, unknown costs or hidden faces disqualify automatic proof.

## Too many payoffs, insufficient setup

An assistant may identify several expensive payoffs but few supported enablers for the agreed plan. Explain the jobs using card text, identify uncertain memberships, and propose a small coordinated package. Preserve interaction and pinned cards. Validate the entire batch, not just each swap separately. If access goals represent setup ingredients, compare those alongside lost capabilities; never optimize a single access percentage or call a lower rate proof that a card should be cut.

## Required recommendation format

State the agreed plan, then at most a few prioritized proposals. For each: current job/problem, candidate or package, cited evidence, why it fits, what is lost, relevant unknowns and validation result. Label strategic fit as assistant judgment. Include a credible alternative when there is a meaningful trade-off. A plan can pass every evaluated floor and still deserve a better-fit proposal.
